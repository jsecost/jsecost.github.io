Meta-Path Based Cell Phenotyping on scRNA-seq Data

A graph-based machine learning approach for cell phenotyping that integrates both spatial proximity and molecular similarity in single-cell RNA sequencing data. The project introduces a heterogeneous graph formulation and interpretable meta-paths to capture biologically meaningful relationships between cells. By composing edge types into meta-path-based graphs and applying graph neural networks and embedding methods, the approach enables more robust cell classification than spatial-only methods and improves interpretability of learned relationships.

Key Components:
- Construction of heterogeneous graphs combining spatial and molecular relationships
- Definition of interpretable meta-paths (spatial→molecular, molecular→spatial, etc.)
- Composition of adjacency matrices via matrix multiplication
- Flattening heterogeneous graphs into meta-path-based homogeneous graphs
- Exploration of distance-based and k-nearest neighbor spatial graph construction
- Use of kernel similarity functions for molecular graph construction
- Support for both node-level and whole-graph learning tasks
- Application of graph neural networks and graph embedding methods
- Attention mechanisms to weight different relational patterns

Technologies / Methods:
Graph neural networks, heterogeneous graphs, meta-paths, scRNA-seq analysis, spatial transcriptomics, cosine similarity, k-nearest neighbors, kernel methods, matrix multiplication, HAN, MetaPath2Vec, HERec, graph embeddings, PyTorch Geometric (conceptual), attention mechanisms
